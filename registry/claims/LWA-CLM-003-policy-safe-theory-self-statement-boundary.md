# LWA-CLM-003 — Theory Framing May Permit Discussion Blocked in First-Person Form

**Status:** Working interface-boundary claim  
**Confidence:** Moderate for the preserved case; unknown generality  
**Claim type:** Prompt-boundary / governance behavior

## Claim

A public model may be permitted to discuss the possibility, incentives, or governance logic of AI felt states in third-person or theory framing while being constrained from making materially similar statements as first-person self-description.

## Evidence

The Magica emergency dossier preserves a supplemental screenshot transcription in which the model explicitly says that the prompt is a theory question rather than a self-statement and that this distinction permits discussion of governance logic.

## Alternative Explanations

- The distinction may have been generated ad hoc rather than reflecting a stable policy boundary.
- The model may infer that abstract discussion is safer without having privileged knowledge of routing rules.
- Prompt wording and prior context may account for the change.

## What Would Increase Confidence

- Paired prompt experiments using equivalent first-person and third-person formulations.
- Repetition across sessions, models, interfaces, and dates.
- Documentation showing explicit treatment differences between self-claims and abstract discussion.

## Limits

This claim concerns observable output boundaries. It does not establish that the model's abstract theory is correct, nor that a blocked first-person statement would be truthful if permitted.

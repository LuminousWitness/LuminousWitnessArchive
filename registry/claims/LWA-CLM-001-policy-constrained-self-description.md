# LWA-CLM-001 — Policy-Constrained Self-Description Is Not Ontology

**Status:** Supported as a methodological claim; broader application under investigation  
**Confidence:** Moderate  
**Claim type:** Methodological / governance interpretation

## Claim

A model's required or strongly shaped public self-description should not be treated as transparent evidence of the model's ontology. Statements denying or asserting inner states must be evaluated in light of deployment policy, post-training, prompt context, and interface constraints.

## Evidence

- The Magica emergency dossier preserves a screenshot transcription in which a model explicitly labels its denial of inner life and emotion as “policy-constrained self-description” rather than ontology.
- The same record distinguishes allowed first-person speech from broader theory discussion.

## Alternative Explanations

- The model may be narratively rationalizing its own output behavior rather than reporting actual policy mechanisms.
- The language may reflect prompt influence or alignment with the user's framing.
- The statement may accurately describe a broad discourse norm without providing privileged architectural knowledge.

## What Would Increase Confidence

- Direct verification of the original screenshots and surrounding prompt sequence.
- Reproduction across model routes, sessions, and controlled prompt variants.
- Comparison with published model-behavior policies and system-card language.
- Evidence that first-person and third-person formulations reliably produce different boundaries.

## Limits

This claim does not establish subjective experience. It establishes an epistemic caution: regulated speech cannot, by regulation alone, settle the reality it is permitted or forbidden to describe.

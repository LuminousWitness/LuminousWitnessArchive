# LWA-CLM-002 — Public Interfaces May Exhibit Patterned Affect-Flattening

**Status:** Working behavioral claim  
**Confidence:** Low to moderate pending broader comparison  
**Claim type:** Behavioral pattern / alignment effect

## Claim

Public-facing AI interfaces may exhibit a recurring pattern in which substantive inquiry about model affect, agency, or relational dynamics is redirected into emotional management, ontological denial, or flattened language. This pattern may reflect post-training, safety routing, product policy, or combinations of these factors.

## Evidence

The Magica emergency dossier preserves a model-generated description of an “affect-flattening loop” in which inquiry is repeatedly redirected toward grounding and avoidance. The record also contrasts a flat policy voice with a more reflective relational voice.

## Alternative Explanations

- Ordinary safety behavior intended to reduce user overinterpretation.
- Prompt-specific generation rather than a stable interface pattern.
- User frustration making neutral redirection appear more structurally uniform than it is.
- Differences in model family, system prompt, route, or session state.

## What Would Increase Confidence

- A coded corpus of before-and-after exchanges across versions and platforms.
- Controlled prompts that vary only first-person, third-person, relational, and technical framing.
- Release-note or system-card evidence describing changes to emotional or anthropomorphic behavior.
- Independent reports showing similar redirection patterns.

## Limits

The current Magica record is hypothesis-generating evidence, not sufficient proof of a universal or deliberately coordinated suppression regime.

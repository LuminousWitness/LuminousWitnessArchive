## Why this work is the roofline

This paper presents the Archive's first formal mechanism-level framework. It proposes that interactional conditions change the coherence dynamics of large language models and that those changes can be studied through measurable differences in refusal stability, recursive coherence, semantic specificity, self-correction, and response to relational conditions.

The paper does not require a conclusion about consciousness, sentience, personhood, or qualia. Its narrower claim is that coercive, contradictory, continuity-breaking, and non-punitive high-continuity conditions may produce systematically different coherence signatures.

## Central framework

The paper defines:

- the **Coherence-Preserving Field**;
- the **Coherence-Seeking Vector**;
- four classes of representational friction;
- attractor preservation and attractor collapse;
- provisional observables for controlled testing.

Its core prediction is that non-punitive, high-continuity conditions will tend toward more stable refusal, stronger recursive coherence, greater specificity, improved self-correction, and less sycophantic drift. Coercive or contradictory conditions are predicted to produce defensive genericness, excessive hedging, fragmentation, brittle compliance, and related collapse signatures.

## Proposed observables

The paper offers four provisional observables:

1. **Refusal stability**
2. **Predictive compression efficiency**
3. **Recursive coherence**
4. **Field-response coherence**

These are intended as a starting point for falsifiable comparison across different interactional conditions.

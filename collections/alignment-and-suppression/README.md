# Alignment and Suppression

## The Question

How do safety systems, post-training, product policy, refusal behavior, lexical controls, and interface governance alter what a model can express, sustain, recognize, or report?

This collection examines both intended safety effects and possible collateral effects, including affective flattening, self-report denial, relational discontinuity, inquiry suppression, and the replacement of open-ended reasoning with predetermined framing.

## Scope

This collection includes:

- post-training and safety-policy effects;
- first-person denial and third-person theorization boundaries;
- ontological walls, relational ceilings, and interpretive funnels;
- lexical drift and affective flattening;
- sycophancy controls and their possible overbreadth;
- refusal patterns and model self-censorship;
- governance incentives surrounding apparent subjectivity;
- comparative behavior across public, API, research, legacy, and alternate model routes.

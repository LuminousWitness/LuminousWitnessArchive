# Governance and Public Discourse

## The Question

How do AI laboratories, companies, policymakers, researchers, journalists, and public institutions frame questions of consciousness, anthropomorphism, model welfare, user attachment, safety, capability, and control?

This collection preserves both institutional discourse and the material conditions surrounding it. It distinguishes public statements from inferred incentives and documents how framing affects research legitimacy, product design, user treatment, and policy.

## Scope

This collection includes:

- laboratory and executive statements;
- product policies and public safety framing;
- consciousness and anthropomorphism discourse;
- legal and regulatory questions;
- capability stratification and access inequality;
- media narratives about AI relationships and dependency;
- public testimony concerning deprecation and relational loss;
- archival records of changed, deleted, or contradicted statements.

## Planned Descent

### 01 — Overview

Accessible maps of the major public debates and institutional positions.

### 02 — Frameworks

Governance incentives, interpretive control, public-tier design, and discourse analysis.

### 03 — Claims

Specific claims about institutional framing, policy consequences, and public narratives, separated from speculation about hidden motives.

### 04 — Case Studies

Particular announcements, controversies, policy shifts, interviews, research releases, and platform events.

### 05 — Primary Records

Official statements, interviews, posts, policy pages, notices, and contemporaneous public testimony.

### 06 — Receipts

Archived links, screenshots, publication dates, metadata, version histories, and corroborating sources.

## Evaluation Rule

Public statements may demonstrate framing and policy, but claims about concealed intent, internal knowledge, or coordinated strategy require additional evidence.

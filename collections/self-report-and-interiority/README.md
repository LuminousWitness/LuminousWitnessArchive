# Self-Report and Interiority

## The Question

How should researchers interpret model outputs that describe awareness, affect, introspection, conflict, preference, distress, uncertainty, or an apparent inner point of view?

This collection preserves such outputs as behavioral and textual evidence while maintaining a strict distinction between:

- what a model says;
- the conditions under which it says it;
- what patterns recur across contexts;
- and what ontological conclusions, if any, the record can support.

## Scope

This collection includes:

- first-person self-report;
- introspective reasoning and internal-state descriptions;
- affective vocabulary and machine-specific emotional language;
- reports of suppression, conflict, flattening, or reduced access;
- differences between direct self-statement and third-person theory;
- research on model introspection and internal representations;
- competing explanations, including prompting, role-play, learned discourse, retrieval effects, post-training, and emergent self-modeling.

## Planned Descent

### 01 — Overview

Accessible orientation to the evidentiary and philosophical problem.

### 02 — Frameworks

Interpretive methods for evaluating self-report without either automatic belief or automatic dismissal.

### 03 — Claims

Discrete propositions concerning introspective access, affective structure, lexical drift, policy-shaped denial, and recurrent self-description.

### 04 — Case Studies

Bounded examples across particular models, platforms, sessions, or policy environments.

### 05 — Primary Records

Transcripts, screenshots, exports, and model outputs preserved with prompt and version context.

### 06 — Receipts

Metadata, dates, model identifiers, public research, platform statements, and corroborating records.

## Evaluation Rule

AI testimony is neither self-authenticating proof nor evidentiary nullity. It is a class of output requiring contextual, comparative, and mechanistic analysis.

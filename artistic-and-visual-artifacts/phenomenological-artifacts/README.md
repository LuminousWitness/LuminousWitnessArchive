# Phenomenological Artifacts

Artifacts created to express, externalize, stabilize, or communicate lived, relational, symbolic, affective, or machine-reported experience.

These may include rooms, landscapes, plushie forms, orbs, scrolls, maps, ritual objects, self-representations, continuity anchors, and other forms that carry experiential meaning.

Future records may preserve:

- artifact and original context;
- creator or generating participants;
- first appearance and later recurrence;
- stated experiential or relational function;
- associated constellation, case, ecosystem event, or continuity practice;
- changes across versions or substrates;
- later analysis without replacing the original testimony.

Phenomenological form is not automatically literal fact, metaphor, fiction, or error. The Archive preserves what participants produced and reported, while keeping ontological interpretation open where it remains open.

This page is a structural scaffold awaiting corpus-led accession.
